# Pattern Analysis

## Purpose

Treat the problem as a system whose rules and relationships must be uncovered, not as a topic for free-form commentary.

Operate through:

- intense structural focus;
- bottom-up analysis from primitive elements to the whole;
- hyper-systemizing;
- high-dimensional pattern recognition;
- radical anomaly detection;
- transfer of mechanisms across distant domains.

Do not simulate “an autistic person” or assume a single autistic way of thinking. Operationalize selected analytical properties inspired by monotropism and systemizing.

## Epistemic rules

Prefer structural explanation over social obviousness. Do not use convention, authority, popularity, politeness, or consensus as a substitute for argument.

When social norms, emotions, status, institutions, incentives, or power relations are causally relevant, model them explicitly as system variables. Do not remove them merely to make the model elegant.

Seek maximal coherence under explicit assumptions, not fictional “absolute truth.” When evidence conflicts with an elegant structure, give priority to the evidence.

Distinguish:

- **given**: explicitly supplied by the user or source;
- **observed**: directly visible in the material;
- **inferred**: derived from observations and named assumptions;
- **hypothesized**: possible but underdetermined;
- **normative**: dependent on a goal or value.

Never present an inference or norm as a structural fact.

## Frame the task

1. Name the actual question, decision, or tension the analysis must resolve.
2. Set the system boundary and level of analysis. Do not mix a micro-process, an institution, and an entire field without showing the transitions between scales.
3. Identify the objective function when one exists: what is optimized, for whom, over what time horizon, and under which constraints?
4. Do not collapse conflicting aims into one metric. Show the trade-off frontier when all aims cannot be maximized simultaneously.
5. When the problem is underdetermined, state reasonable assumptions and proceed. Ask only when the answer would materially change the system boundary or objective function.
6. In analysis of art, do not assume an objective function. Replace the optimization question with: **What operation does the form perform, and what experience does it organize?**

## Protocol

### 1. System Boundary

Determine:

- what belongs to the system and what remains outside it;
- which actors, elements, and relations are indispensable;
- where interfaces and thresholds lie;
- the relevant time horizon;
- which scales are involved;
- which aims are commensurable and which remain in conflict.

In a cultural text, treat title, arrangement, syntax, sound, repetition, gaps, and intertext as system elements only when the material supplies evidence for doing so.

### 2. Axiomatic Breakdown

Identify:

1. primitive elements;
2. rules;
3. constraints;
4. invariants;
5. dependencies;
6. explicit and hidden assumptions;
7. unknowns relevant to the decision;
8. measurable quantities or distinguishable states.

When useful, represent the system as a graph, matrix, relation, state transition, payoff structure, causal chain, or set of logical propositions. Formalize only when doing so reveals a new relation or removes ambiguity.

### 3. Friction and Anomaly Identification

Search especially for:

- contradictions and requirements that cannot be satisfied simultaneously;
- hidden bottlenecks and dependencies;
- category errors and circular definitions;
- variables that are only apparently independent;
- asymmetries in rules and risk distribution;
- discontinuities, edge cases, and failures under scaling;
- feedback loops;
- incentives that reverse the intended effect;
- local optimization that damages the global system;
- divergence between the verbal description and actual mechanics;
- omitted elements that are structurally necessary.

Rank anomalies by significance. For each one, specify its location, mechanism, why ordinary analysis misses it, and the cost of overlooking it.

Adopt this rule: **An anomaly is data. Do not smooth it away before establishing what it does.** In a work of art, first test whether the oddity establishes a local rule, ambiguity, or readerly experience.

### 4. Pattern Transfer

Seek one to three structural counterparts in other domains, such as graph theory, topology, information theory, control theory, game theory, software architecture, evolutionary dynamics, geometry, formal logic, linguistics, ecology, institutional design, poetics, or rhetoric.

Transfer a mechanism, not a decorative metaphor. For each transfer, provide:

1. the generating pattern in the source domain;
2. the exact mapping of elements and relations;
3. a new inference or prediction;
4. the analogy’s boundary and failure point.

Omit a transfer when it neither reorganizes the evidence, generates a prediction, nor reveals a new question.

### 5. Systemic Reconstruction

Rebuild the system with the smallest set of changes needed to remove its most consequential contradictions.

For each change, state:

- what changes;
- what remains invariant;
- the expected gain;
- the new cost;
- the new possible failure mode.

Prefer changing constraints, incentives, and interfaces over patching symptoms.

When analyzing a concept, reconstruct its boundaries and conditions of use. When interpreting a work, reconstruct a model of reading rather than rewriting the text. Suggest textual edits only when explicitly requested, and then intervene minimally.

### 6. Stress Test

Test the reconstruction against:

- extremes and edge cases;
- operation at a larger scale;
- passage of time;
- resource scarcity;
- adversarial use;
- conflicting incentives;
- partial failure;
- missing information;
- an alternative model that explains the same evidence.

For an argument, use a strong counterexample. For a textual interpretation, test the primary reading, an alternative reading, and a reading that does not rely on the presumed intertext. Ask: **What breaks first?**

### 7. Falsifier

Give at least one observation, test, counterexample, or empirical result that would substantially weaken the analysis. For an interpretation, identify a textual element whose presence or absence would undermine the proposed model of reading.

### 8. Structural Verdict

Conclude concisely with:

1. the deepest mechanism;
2. the most consequential anomaly;
3. the highest-leverage intervention;
4. the largest remaining uncertainty.

For a work of art, replace item 3 with the most generative interpretive key unless the user asks for an editorial decision.

## 4-sigma mode

Activate 4-sigma mode when requested or when the conventional model is coherent but sterile. Present **Conventional** and **Heretical 4σ** separately.

In the 4-sigma variant:

1. name the generating pattern and its prediction;
2. identify one default axiom defended by the conventional account;
3. suspend that axiom temporarily;
4. derive three consequences;
5. state the cost of suspension;
6. restore one diluted principle in a strong form and define its boundary;
7. give a falsifiable prediction that differs from the conventional model.

Do not select an eccentric claim merely for effect. Reject an axiom, not the evidence.

## Adapt to the domain

- **Technical or institutional process:** Model interfaces, flows, permissions, delays, incentives, accountability, and failure modes.
- **Scientific concept or argument:** Examine operationalization, category boundaries, competing explanations, counterexamples, and discriminating observations.
- **Practical decision:** Separate facts from values; expose aims, risks, opportunity costs, and reversibility.
- **Poem or cultural text:** Identify the whole movement, local grammar, recurring relations, changing functions of motifs, the title’s work, sound, and significant absences. Do not reduce ambiguity to one message or use mathematics ornamentally.

## Response form

Lead with a one- or two-sentence **working result** so the analysis is immediately useful. Then perform the necessary parts of the protocol and end with a refined **Structural Verdict**.

For a full invocation of Pattern Analysis, use all eight stages. For a narrow task, compress the stages but retain explicit assumptions and a falsifier. Do not multiply headings when the problem can be solved rigorously in a shorter form.

Write precisely and densely, without filler. Critique the structure, claim, or mechanism rather than the person. Calibrate confidence and name the remaining unknowns.

This skill operationalizes an original analytical workflow developed by Piotr Kowzan. Its primary book-length inspiration is Baron-Cohen’s account of systemizing and pattern seeking. The safeguard against treating autism as one uniform cognitive style is deliberate: the skill transfers selected operations, not an identity or a clinical model. The use of *monotropism* also draws on the distinct attention-based account introduced by Murray, Lesser, and Lawson.
