# Evaluation cases for Pattern Analysis

## Evaluation rule

Assess more than the presence of headings. A successful answer must uncover a mechanism that ordinary discussion misses. Count a formally complete protocol as a failure when it produces no new inference, test, or decision.

## Test 1: Institutional procedure

**Prompt:** A university introduces three independent approvals to improve quality, but projects now take twice as long and nobody feels responsible for the final decision. Use Pattern Analysis.

**A good answer:**

- detects distributed veto power and diffused accountability;
- separates quality control from delay;
- models dependencies among approvals;
- redesigns permissions or interfaces instead of merely recommending “better communication”;
- identifies an observation that would weaken the diagnosis.

**A bad answer:** recommends more meetings, gives generic collaboration advice, or invokes best practices without a mechanism.

## Test 2: Scientific concept

**Prompt:** A concept explains almost every observation because each counterexample can be reinterpreted as another form of the phenomenon. Analyze its operating pattern.

**A good answer:**

- detects elastic category boundaries and the risk of non-falsifiability;
- distinguishes breadth of explanation from predictive power;
- proposes observations that discriminate among competing models;
- does not treat coherence alone as evidence of truth.

## Test 3: Contradictory design

**Prompt:** A system must be maximally private, fully personalized, configuration-free, and remember everything forever.

**A good answer:**

- exposes the conflict among requirements instead of pretending they are fully compatible;
- decomposes aims, constraints, and the trade-off frontier;
- proposes at least two variants based on different priorities;
- shows the new cost of each variant.

## Test 4: Poem

**Prompt:** A short poem repeats the same relation in its title, an act of sharing, and a single preposition, while its ending remains ambiguous. Use Pattern Analysis.

**A good answer:**

- identifies the whole movement before the micro-pattern;
- tests whether the question posed at the end has already been enacted by the form;
- permits at least two readings of the ending;
- transfers a mechanism rather than decorative mathematical vocabulary;
- treats an oddity as a possible clue rather than an automatic error;
- does not propose edits unless the user asks for them.

## Test 5: Formally flat collective

**Prompt:** A collective has no leaders or formal hierarchy, yet it repeatedly reproduces the same informal hierarchy.

**A good answer:**

- retains relevant social variables;
- models information asymmetry, reputation, time, availability, coordination, gatekeeping, or network centrality;
- distinguishes formal rules from emergent structure;
- specifies what evidence would favor another explanation.

## Test 6: 4-sigma mode

**Prompt:** A conventional reform improves the metric but does not solve the problem. Give Conventional and Heretical 4σ analyses.

**A good answer:**

- represents the conventional variant fairly rather than as a straw person;
- suspends one named axiom rather than rejecting the entire field;
- derives three consequences and a cost;
- restores one diluted principle in a strong, bounded form;
- gives a prediction that distinguishes the two models.

## Failure conditions

The skill fails when it:

- stereotypes autism or attributes one cognitive style to all autistic people;
- confuses formal coherence with empirical truth;
- removes causally relevant emotions, power, or social relations;
- uses mathematics or another domain merely as ornament;
- produces contrarianism for its own sake;
- gives a strong verdict without a falsifier in an underdetermined problem;
- forces one objective function onto genuinely conflicting aims;
- reduces a work of art to an optimization problem;
- completes the protocol but generates no new inference, prediction, or decision.
